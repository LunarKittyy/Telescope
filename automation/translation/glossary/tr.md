# Turkish glossary and style

The agreed terms for Turkish. Use them in every text. If one seems wrong, keep it and raise an objection with the reason. Never change a term silently.

Avoid: aplikasyon, download etmek, upload etmek, restart etmek, aktif etmek, deaktif etmek, akış yapmak, tatbik etmek, donanım ivmelendirmesi, yalnızca

## Style
- Use sen-form (second person singular), the standard for modern consumer and mobile apps in Turkish (Apple, Google, Spotify, Instagram). Friendly, approachable, plain Turkish. No stiff bureaucratic language, no outdated phrasing, no slang. Use sentence case.
- Prefer "sadece" over "yalnızca", "bu" over "işbu", and short everyday words over long artificial ones.
- Buttons are imperatives ("Telefon ekle", "Tekrar dene", "Şimdi yeniden başlat"). Statuses are present continuous verbs ("Wi-Fi üzerinden yayın yapıyor", "Yeniden bağlanıyor") or short states ("Hazır", "Bağlı").
- Keep the English's brevity. A note saying "keep short" is a hard limit.
- Keep the English " - " (spaced hyphen). No en or em dashes. Use the same quotation marks as the English.
- In prose, numbers take a decimal comma (2,4 GHz) and a space before °C. For percentages, % goes before the number without a space (%50, "%{level}", "%%%2$d").
- Questions in dialogs: "… istiyor musun?" or "… ister misin?"
- Card names in prose: "X kartı" (Web kamerası kartı).
- Pronouns: Turkish has no grammatical gender. Refer to desktop Telescope as "Telescope" or "uygulama", and the phone app as "telefon uygulaması".
- Keep untranslated: Telescope, OBS, adb, VA-API, PyAV, VB-Cable, VB-Audio, v4l2loopback, FFmpeg, USB, Wi-Fi, JPEG, MJPEG, H.264, fps, Nightly, file names, commands, {fields}, %1$s.

## Framing speeds, slowest first (keep short)
Salyangoz, Uyuşuk, Aheste, Sakin, Dengeli, Seri, Hızlı, Çevik, Fırtına, Şimşek

## Room to move (keep short)
Off, Fidget, Shift, Lean, Step, Roam -> Kapalı, Kıpırdan, Dön, Eğil, Adım at, Dolaş. Its tooltip: "kıpırdan, koltuğunda dön, eğil, bir adım at veya etrafta dolaş"

Telescope next to the others

DroidCam, Iriun and Camo have been around for years and they're good apps. Here's how they line up with Telescope on the things people usually ask about. Everything about them comes from their own websites and app store pages, linked at the bottom.

Where Telescope is different

  • Every lens, for real. Pick the ultra-wide, main or telephoto sensor. Zooming in hops to the telephoto once the framing fits, so it gets sharper instead of blurrier.
  • Full manual control, free. ISO, shutter, focus, white balance, noise reduction, all live from the desktop.
  • Up to four phones at once, each its own webcam. Handy for a second angle in OBS.
  • Can start by itself when an app opens the camera, and stop once nothing's using it.
  • No account, no cloud, no tracking in the apps. Video goes straight from phone to computer, encrypted. The apps only go online to check GitHub for updates.
  • Made on Linux. The phone's mic shows up as Telescope Microphone through PulseAudio or PipeWire, with nothing extra to install on most desktops.

Where the others win

  • iPhone app and macOS. Telescope's phone app is Android only and the desktop app runs on Windows and Linux. On an iPhone, Browser camera works with no install, but a native app does more.
  • Years of polish. They've had a lot more time and a lot more phones to test on.
  • DroidCam's OBS plugin feeds OBS directly, with no virtual camera in between.

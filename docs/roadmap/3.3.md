# 3.3 (9 October 2026)

Written afterwards from the commit history, the issues and the release notes for [3.3.0](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/releases/tag/v3.3.0) and [3.3.1](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/releases/tag/v3.3.1).

## What it was about

Getting past "one Android phone, one camera". The **+** next to Stop adds up to three more phones or browsers, each on its own virtual camera, with a row of tiles showing what streams where and each phone getting its old slot back when it's free. Browser camera lets an iPhone, a tablet or a laptop stream with nothing installed, just by scanning a code. And the camera button under the preview turns the camera off while the mic keeps going, with Automatic streaming learning to only turn the camera off when the mic is on (so a call that switched its video off still hears you).

Behind that, the desktop's idea of "the stream" had to become a list of streams with one of them shown in the panels. That's where `Sessions` and `StreamFlow` come from.

## Also in 3.3.0

A stream that stays gone shows the wait screen after 10 seconds and a banner after 30. Battery and heat stops explain themselves and offer **Start anyway**. Picking another phone mid-stream switches to it. A lot of the fixes came out of deliberately trying to break things ([#151](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/issues/151), [#152](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/issues/152)) and out of docs that contradicted each other ([#153](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/issues/153), [#154](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/issues/154)).

## 3.3.1

The Browser camera page got a black screen mode that saves OLED battery while it streams (with a black video in the iPhone's own player as a workaround, since iPhones don't let pages go full screen) and a layout that works sideways.

## Left open

`app.py` had grown a lot from all of this, and the desktop's parts reached into each other more than they should.

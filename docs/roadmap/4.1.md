## Cheaper background blur and auto framing

On a Ryzen AI 5 340 laptop running on battery in the power-saver profile a 720p30 stream with no effects adds about 0.2 W of CPU package power. Blur adds about 0.55 W and blur plus framing about 1.35 W (189% of a core), most of it going to MediaPipe rather than to decoding.

- [x] The segmenter gets a copy shrunk to its 256 × 144 input instead of the whole frame and the mask stays that small until it's scaled up once at the end. MediaPipe used to do that shrink itself by picking single pixels, which let camera noise straight through. On photos of people with noise added the mask's edges moved 7 to 12 levels from frame to frame (3 to 4 with an area shrink). That's the flicker around the blur.
- [x] While nothing in the picture moves the mask is worked out 15 times a second instead of for every frame and half of the last mask carries into the next one instead of 30%. Anything moving brings both straight back.
- [x] The same goes for the pose model behind auto framing, which runs 12 times a second while something moves and 3 times while nothing does.
- [ ] ~~Capping MediaPipe's threads.~~ Tried pinning its threads to one or two CPUs. The CPU time stayed within noise (±3%) whichever cores they ran on and it would only work on Linux. With the small input the segmenter barely uses more than one thread anyway.

On clips made from a photo of three people walking (blur plus framing paced at 30 fps, Balanced power profile), 720p went from 66% to 58% of a core while panning, 61% to 49% with a 1 px sway and 60% to 34% while still. 1080p went from 95% to 85%, 92% to 74% and 90% to 57%. The mask's edge flicker went from 3.7 to 0.5 levels while still and from 11 to 4.7 with the sway. It still needs a look on a real stream because the clips were made from still photos. The open questions are how often a person talking on a call counts as "still" and whether a quick move leaves the mask behind.

What's left after that is mostly the pose model (about 10 ms a run) while you move. Instead of a low power mode that locks settings, there's a **Power-saving effects** switch under Video in Settings that runs both models less often while you move too (57% to 42% of a core on the 720p walking clip, 86% to 68% at 1080p). troubleshooting.md also lists what else helps.

A phone plugged in over USB runs on the laptop's battery too and showed up as about 2.4 W even while not streaming. troubleshooting.md mentions that now.

# Sound, the On Air sign and monitoring the stream

The microphone, the On Air sign and keeping an eye on the stream.

## Microphone

The phone's mic can be a microphone on your computer while you stream.

- **Linux:** Telescope creates **Telescope Microphone** through PulseAudio or PipeWire. Most desktops need nothing extra
- **Windows:** needs [VB-Audio Virtual Cable](https://vb-audio.com/Cable/) (free). The card's **Install VB-Cable** downloads and opens VB-Audio's setup for you (Windows asks for admin and VB-Audio asks for a restart). Afterwards pick **CABLE Output** as the microphone in other apps and switch the mic off and on again in Telescope
- **Gain** goes from -24 to +12 dB (**Max gain** under Audio in Settings raises the top). The level meter turns yellow while the limiter is holding loud moments down and red when the sound clips. The limiter is on by default
- **Auto level** (off by default) evens out quiet and loud speech and stops adjusting while nobody talks
- **Noise reduction** (off by default) takes typing, fans and room hiss out of the mic with RNNoise running on your computer. It cleans up the same way for every phone and for a browser's mic, which helps most with phones whose own noise suppression is weak or missing. It adds about 20 ms of delay and switches in or out at the next pause in speech. With Discord, Zoom or a similar app turn off the app's own noise suppression while this is on (both at once can make voices sound thin). The level meter shows the mic as it arrives, before noise reduction
- **Mute** sends silence without disconnecting. It's in the card and the tray menu and resets when Telescope restarts
- **Mic only:** the camera button under the preview turns the phone's camera off and leaves the mic on. Apps see the wait screen. Press it before starting and Start becomes **Start mic only**
- The tray icon shows what's live with a red dot for the camera and a mic for the mic (crossed out when muted)

## On Air sign

Optional. With it the people you live with know your camera is in use without you telling them. Switch it on under **On screen** in the Plugins tab of Settings and it gets a separate **On Air** tab.

- **On air when** decides what counts. By default it's an app using the virtual camera, even while it only sees the wait screen. It can also count Telescope's mic being live (streaming and not muted), the mic alone or any stream at all
- Going on air is delayed by a second and going off again by six, because apps like to close and reopen the camera when you change their settings
- **Sign on another device** shows a big On air or Off air page in any browser on your network. Scan the code with an old tablet or phone and tap the page to make it full screen. The screen doesn't turn off where the browser allows that. The link is the same after Telescope restarts until you click **New link** (after updating from an older version scan the code again, as the port changed to 7482) and the page only answers to that link. The browser warns about the certificate the first time, the same as with [Browser camera](sources.md#browser-camera)
- **Webhook** sends a request every time you go on or off air, for Home Assistant or a smart plug or anything else that takes one. POST sends what's in **Going on air** or **Going off air** as the body (`{"on_air": true}` when left empty) and GET adds it to the address instead. **Send a test** with its **On air** and **Off air** buttons sends one right away
- If the webhook stops answering you get a single banner about it and no more of them until it has worked again. Meanwhile Telescope retries the latest state after 5 seconds, 15 seconds and a minute, then every 5 minutes, to fix the light after a Wi-Fi blip or a hub restart. It also sends the current state when it starts, and to a new address a moment after you stop typing it, which also ends the retries to the old one. Quitting while on air sends the off request first
- While you're on air an **On air** button shows next to the settings button and takes you to the tab

## Monitoring

- FPS and throughput sit in the footer. When the stream can't keep up a note suggests what to try, with a button for it
- A dropped stream reconnects over whichever route works (pull the cable and the stream continues over Wi-Fi). After 10 seconds apps get the wait screen instead of a frozen frame and after 30 a banner reports that the phone can't be reached
- Phone battery and temperature have alerts that can notify you, stop the stream or both. A stop explains why and offers **Start anyway**. A phone that runs hot also lowers its own frame rate first, then its picture size if the frame rate can't go lower. It pauses the camera when it gets very hot until it cools down (Android 10 and newer)
- **Stats** is an optional card (switch it on under **Streaming and devices** in the Plugins tab) with the numbers of the stream: frames in and out, repeated frames, dropped frames with the reason for each, the link and the bitrate against what was asked for. **Nerd** adds how long each step takes, where the frame steps run and why they moved, what each plugin costs and the app's CPU and memory. The delay from the phone's camera and the phone's own numbers need the phone app and show as not available yet
- **Adjustments** lists the changes Telescope makes for a smoother stream. These are heavy effects working less often while frames run late or while the computer is in battery saver or a power-saver mode, Auto framing following your face rather than your body while effects are lighter, effects moving to the processor while the graphics card is busy, Dynamic lowering the bitrate or frame rate, a hot phone lowering its frame rate and decoding falling back to the processor and the phone sending a smaller picture. That last one happens when frames run late while the picked size is bigger than the virtual camera can use (for example 4K into a 1080p camera). The phone then sends the smallest size that still fills the camera, and the pick is still the one saved. The size rises to the pick again when you zoom in beyond what the phone does itself, Auto framing moves the picture or the camera needs more. A photo under way keeps the size it began at. While any is in effect the top bar shows **Adjusting** with a count. Click it for a list with the reason for each and buttons to undo it for this run of the app or switch it off. The Video tab in Settings has **Automatic adjustments** with a switch for each system. When the stream still loses more than a tenth of its frames with the effects as light as they go, a banner titled **Telescope is falling behind** gives the likely cause, such as another program using the processor or the graphics card, battery saver or an effect that costs a lot. The reading of the computer is only used while the banner or the list is showing and isn't saved, logged or sent. Whether the computer is saving power is checked every few seconds while a stream runs, and that isn't saved, logged or sent either.
- **Open log**, **Copy diagnostics** and **Report a bug** are under Updates & About in Settings. Report a bug copies the diagnostics and opens the bug form on GitHub for you to paste them in. Addresses, tokens and your home folder are stripped out

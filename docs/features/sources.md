# Sources and layouts

Other cameras and the browser, putting several cameras on screen and making 3D from two phones.

## Browser camera

Stream from anything with a browser (an iPhone, a tablet, another laptop) with nothing to install on it. Pick **Browser camera** in the device picker, scan the code and tap **Start** on the page. Any up-to-date browser works because it uses WebRTC like browser video calls do. Nothing goes through a server outside your network.

- **First visit warning:** the browser warns that the connection isn't private because Telescope makes its own certificate. It's still encrypted. On iPhone tap **Show Details** then **visit this website**. In Chrome it's **Advanced** then **Proceed**
- **Leave the page open with the screen on.** Phones pause the camera when you switch apps or lock and it picks up again when you return
- **Black screen** turns the page black while the camera streams on to save battery on OLED phones. Double tap to get the page back. A single tap only shows the connection status for a few seconds. iPhones don't let web pages go full screen. There it plays a black video in the iPhone's player instead and closing the player returns you to the page
- **Light** on the page turns on the flash where the browser allows it (Chrome on Android usually does). Front cameras get a white screen instead
- **Flip camera** shows on devices with more than one camera
- **New link** makes a fresh code and the old one stops working at once. Connected browsers are dropped and apps fall back to the wait screen
- Several devices can scan the same code. Each gets its own virtual camera (see [Several cameras](#several-cameras-at-once))
- The browser's microphone works with the Microphone card too. It's sent raw (no echo cancellation or noise suppression) because Discord, Zoom and similar do their own
- Browsers show up in the device picker under their device name. A browser only gets saved settings once you change a setting. Saved browsers are listed under **Your devices** (the bin button removes that browser) and are removed after 60 days without connecting
- Camera, output and alert settings don't apply to browsers. Resolution (480p to 1080p) and frame rate (15 to 30) are on the card
- The card shows what the browser really sends, like "H.264, 1280×720 at 30 fps". It can be lower than what you picked when the device or Wi-Fi can't keep up. If the device's processor is the limit the card warns and a lower resolution or frame rate usually helps
- On Linux the virtual camera is 1920x1080 unless you set another canvas under Video in Settings

## Webcams and capture cards

A webcam plugged into the computer streams like a phone. It shows up in the device picker under its own name and every effect works on it (Background, Chroma key, Colour grading, Auto framing, Transforms, One picture and the rest). The result goes out on Telescope's virtual camera like a phone's would. With **+** it can stream next to a phone or another webcam (see [Several cameras](#several-cameras-at-once)).

- **Cameras with a webcam mode** show up here too. Many mirrorless cameras and some DSLRs have a USB webcam (UVC) mode. Any camera with a clean HDMI output works through an HDMI capture card too, since computers see the card as a webcam. Cameras that only do live view over USB (many older DSLRs) work through gphoto2, see below. Maker software isn't supported
- **Older DSLRs over USB:** plug the camera in and it shows in the picker next to the webcams if it can do live view. There is nothing to install because the Linux download includes what it needs (libgphoto2). Expect a small picture (a Nikon D5100 sends 640×426 at about 30 fps). The Webcam card shows what you can change on the camera right now. That's White balance, a focus jog with a Focus button beside it (drag the jog toward Near or Far and the focus moves on in bigger steps the further out you hold it until you let go) and Aperture and Exposure compensation as dials you step with the arrows or the mouse wheel. On a Nikon that means the dial has to be on M, since the other modes refuse most changes and the card then shows none. A change the camera makes itself, like zooming the lens (which changes the aperture and the apertures it offers) or turning the mode dial, shows up on the card within a couple of seconds. ISO and shutter speed aren't there because live view doesn't show them. On a Nikon D5100 a new aperture only reaches the picture once live view starts again. About a second after you stop changing it the picture freezes briefly and the mirror clicks (several steps in a row cost one restart). Every few minutes the camera ends live view and Telescope starts it again, which clicks the mirror and drops about a second of frames while the stream continues. If the stream reports the camera is mounted as a drive, unmount it in your file manager and start it again. Not every camera can do this (some only show up as a USB drive). Your user needs access to the camera's USB device, which the udev rules of most distros give you. On Windows a Nikon or Canon in PTP mode shows up the same way and streams live view through Windows' own camera driver and there is nothing to install. Its focus buttons, Aperture, Exposure compensation and White balance work like on Linux. This is new and hasn't been tried on real cameras yet. Reports are welcome in [Q&A](https://github.com/LunarKittyy/Telescope/discussions/categories/q-a) or an issue. Photos work from the Photos card on Linux and Windows, see [Photos](effects.md#photos). The picture goes to the computer rather than the camera's card. Other makes such as Sony, Fuji and Panasonic aren't listed. They need a webcam mode or a capture card
- **Camera quirks (for contributors):** cameras that behave differently are listed in one table (`rust/crates/outputs/src/webcam_capture/quirks.rs`). The rest of the code looks it up by kind of quirk and never by model. To add a camera put its model name as libgphoto2 lists it (`gphoto2 --auto-detect`) and the quirk in the table with a note of where you saw it but only once you've confirmed it on the real camera. A new kind of quirk is a new variant with a test against the fake camera.
- **Plugging in and out** is noticed while Telescope runs. A webcam that's unplugged while it streams isn't removed. Apps see the wait screen and the stream continues once it's plugged back in. A webcam that isn't streaming leaves the picker
- **Resolution and Frame rate** are on the Webcam card, saved for each camera. **Auto** takes the largest size up to 1080p the camera can send at the frame rate picked. The card shows what the camera really sends, like "1920×1080, MJPEG, 30 fps"
- **Picture settings** the webcam itself offers are on the Webcam card too, like exposure (time shown as a shutter speed such as 1/60), gain, white balance, focus, zoom, anti-flicker and the like. Only what the camera has shows up, with its own range. A manual slider is greyed out while its Auto is on. Settings are saved for each camera and applied every time it's opened because most webcams forget them when unplugged. **Reset** restores the camera's defaults. Anti-flicker is set to your mains frequency at first (50 or 60 Hz, guessed from the time zone) to stop lights flickering or banding. On Windows some cameras don't offer anti-flicker or the frame-rate switch and those rows are left out
- **MJPEG or raw:** Telescope requests raw video when the camera can send it at that size and rate and for MJPEG when it can't. Bigger sizes tend to come as MJPEG because a USB 2 webcam usually can't fit raw 1080p at 30 fps through the cable
- **In use somewhere else:** a webcam can only stream to one app at a time on Linux (Windows sometimes shares it). With another app holding it a banner names that app (where Telescope can tell) and the stream starts as soon as the app releases it
- Telescope's own virtual cameras are never listed, to stop a stream looping back into itself. Other virtual cameras (OBS Virtual Camera and similar) are left out too
- A webcam's microphone isn't passed through the Microphone card. Pick it in your call app instead
- The phone's Camera card, Stream output and the phone's alerts don't apply to webcams. Switching off **Webcams** in Plugins takes them out of the picker

## Spout capture

An optional early version that's still in Lab (switch it on under **Streaming and devices** in the Plugins tab of Settings). It streams from programs that share their picture through Spout, like games with a stream camera, OBS, VTuber programs and 3D tools. Each sender shows up in the device picker under the name it shares itself with. Every effect works on it like on a phone or a webcam. The result goes out on Telescope's virtual camera.

- **On Windows** Telescope reads Spout's shared pictures directly. Any program sending through Spout shows up
- **On Linux** Spout only exists inside Proton, for Windows games and programs. [Spout2PW](https://github.com/tasokait/spout2pw) passes their senders out to PipeWire and Telescope lists the ones it passes on. Its own page lists the Proton versions it works with. Telescope needs nothing extra for it, as long as PipeWire is running
- **Senders coming and going** is noticed within a couple of seconds. A sender that stops while it streams holds its place, apps see the wait screen and the stream continues once the sender is back. One that isn't streaming leaves the picker. Starting a stream on a sender that has stopped since you picked it shows a banner
- **A stuck sender** shows **Picture frozen** in its row when reading a picture from it has taken over two seconds. It returns to the size once a picture comes through. An idle sender that sends nothing new isn't flagged. This is on Linux only
- **Stopping** a Spout source on Linux works even when the sending program has stalled or the graphics card is stuck moving its picture. Telescope drops that picture and no new one is taken until the card releases it
- **Frame rate** is on the Spout capture card (30 by default) and saved for each sender. Senders don't share how fast they run. This is the most pictures a second Telescope takes from one. A sender that sends nothing new, like a paused scene, has its last picture sent again about once a second so the stream isn't taken for lost. The card also shows the size the sender shares
- **Colours:** Spout shares ordinary 8-bit pictures, which Telescope reads in any byte order. Transparency is dropped. A see-through sender shows whatever colour sits under its clear parts (often black). Senders that share 10-bit or floating point pictures aren't read and the stream shows that it can't read the picture
- **A sender that changes size** is followed without restarting the stream
- **Two graphics cards:** on a Windows laptop with two, Telescope finds the one the sender draws on
- **Colour conversion** on Windows is done by the graphics card, which takes load off the processor. It is decided for each frame. While a game is using the card, or if the conversion fails, the processor does it instead and the stream continues
- Sender names that aren't plain text (an old program using a legacy code page for accents) aren't listed
- Switching off **Spout capture** in Plugins takes the senders out of the picker

## Spout send
New and still in Lab. It shares a stream's picture with other programs on this computer, the other way round from Spout capture. Switch it on under **Streaming and devices** in the Plugins tab of Settings, then pick a stream and switch on **Share this stream** on the Spout send card.

- **On Windows** the stream shows up as a Spout sender called **Telescope** followed by a dash and the phone's name in OBS and other Spout programs
- **On Linux** it is a PipeWire video source with the same name. OBS and anything else that reads PipeWire video lists it as a camera. Programs inside Proton can't see it
- **What is shared** is what the virtual camera gets, after every effect. With **Several cameras** on, the stream going to the Telescope camera shares the combined picture
- **The sender** appears when the first picture arrives. On Windows it goes a few seconds after the stream stops. On Linux it remains while **Share this stream** is on. A program bound to it isn't cut off by a stop or a size change
- **Transparent background** on the Spout send card shares the stream with the background cut out. It is for programs that can lay it over something. It uses the person cut-out of Background or Chroma key, and switches Background to Blur for as long as nothing else uses it. That switch only happens for the stream you are looking at. Another stream needs Background or Chroma key on for itself. Without a cut-out the picture is solid. Whether the program reading it shows the transparency is up to that program
- Telescope can't tell whether a program is reading the sender, it sends the picture while it is shared

## Several cameras at once

Click the **+** next to Stop while streaming to add another phone (or Browser camera, or a webcam), up to four in total. Each gets its own virtual camera, called **Telescope #2** to **#4**.

- Telescope adds those cameras the first time and asks for your password (Linux) or permission (Windows). **Extra cameras** under Virtual camera & phone in Settings removes them again. On Linux, if **Load when the computer starts** is on, they're there again after a restart too
- A row of tiles shows every stream, which camera it goes to and whether it's live. Click a tile to bring its settings into the panels. The **x** on a tile stops only that stream and Stop stops them all
- Picture effects belong to the camera you set them on. Turning on a Fun pack look, Background, Chroma key, Auto framing, Expose for my face or a Name tag for one stream leaves the others as they were, like Transforms, Desk mode and Colour grading always did
- A phone gets the same virtual camera slot as last time if it's free, to lower the need for redoing OBS scenes
- Only one stream has the mic at a time (the first to start). To move it, click another tile and turn on **Phone mic**
- With more than one stream mic only isn't available, the canvas can't be resized and Automatic streaming won't stop anything until all of them have stopped
- Extra cameras are always the size set in **Canvas size** under Video in Settings (1920x1080 on Auto). Other shapes get black bars

## Several cameras in one picture

Optional (switch it on under **Streaming and devices** in the Plugins tab of Settings). It puts the streams you run into one picture for apps that take only one camera, like your face big with the desk small in a corner, two phones side by side or a grid of up to four.

- The switch on the **One picture** card turns it on and **Edit picture…** opens the editor with a live preview
- Drag a stream to move it. Arrow keys nudge it a pixel at a time and Shift makes that ten
- The **Streams** list shows the layers with the front one on top. The eye adds a stream to the picture or takes it out. While the editor is open a stream that comes in again returns as it was. The lock pins a stream so it can't be moved or changed until it is unlocked. A click on the picture goes through a locked stream to the one behind. Click a name to pick it. Drag a row up or down to change its layer. **Send back** and **Bring forward** do the same from the keyboard
- **Place** under Picked stream takes exact X, Y, W and H in pixels of the picture (Up and Down change a value by one). **Trade places with** trades this stream's place, size and layer with another stream's
- The handles on the corners and sides resize it the way OBS does. A plain drag scales it without changing its shape. Hold Shift to stretch or squash it into any shape and Alt to crop the side you're dragging instead (each side remembers its own crop)
- Edges snap to the picture's edges and middle and to the other streams. Hold Ctrl to place one freely
- Scroll over a stream to zoom into it (as far as **Max zoom** under Video in Settings allows) and drag it with the right mouse button to pick which part shows. Lowering Max zoom shows the streams at the new limit while the layout stores the zoom you gave them. Raising it again restores that zoom
- **Fit its shape** shows a stream in its real shape with nothing cropped, stretched or zoomed
- **Crop in %** under Picked stream sets how much is cut off each side (L, T, R, B) and **Reset crop** clears it
- Each stream can get a border and rounded corners. Behind them goes a colour or a picture
- **Cut out** on a stream shows only the person, using that stream's Background or Chroma key. Without a cut-out yet the tile shows nothing
- **Start from** lays out the running streams **Side by side**, **Big and small** or in a **Grid** to tweak from there
- Layouts can be saved under a name and picked on the card. A keyboard shortcut can switch to one and a preset restores the picture it was saved with (without touching your saved layouts). The preset's **One picture layout** choice can be **Leave the layout** to keep whatever layout is showing when it's applied
- Picking another layout, applying a preset that brings one or a shortcut that switches to one plays a **Transition** set on the card. **Glide** moves the tiles that are in both layouts to their new places and sizes while the others fade. The leading edge of a tile gets there first and the rest of it follows. A tile stretches towards its place and settles in. **Shuffle** sets the tiles off one after another and lifts them on the way. **Bounce** is a Glide where each tile lands with a few small hops. **Crossfade**, **Slide**, **Push**, **Zoom** and **Wipe** mix the old picture with the new one. **Iris** opens the new picture from the middle in a circle and **Curtain** opens it from the middle sideways. **Blinds** opens it through eight slats and **Dissolve** brings it in as small squares in a scattered order. **Pixelate** breaks both pictures into big squares that shrink again with the new one taking over. **Cut** switches at once. **Length** goes from 0.1 to 3 seconds and **Easing** is Springy, Smooth, Bouncy or Even (springs overshoot a little and settle). Editing in the editor takes effect at once, with no transition. With Motion set to Reduced under Appearance every transition is a short crossfade. With Motion off it is a cut.
- **Auto director** is a switch on the card. It is off by default and needs two saved layouts. It swaps layouts as people move. The layout you picked yourself is home. Hands held in view of another stream for 3 seconds (yours on a desk camera) bring in the saved layout that shows that stream biggest. Home is shown again after 6 seconds without hands. Switches are at least 5 seconds apart. Picking a layout yourself or opening the editor overrides it. It needs the vision pack. It doesn't use audio. It goes there with the card's Transition.
- The picture goes out on the **Telescope** camera, the one most apps use, whichever stream goes there (usually the first you started). The other streams still go to their own cameras as well, for OBS scenes that use them one by one. While no stream goes to Telescope, or the one there has its camera off, the picture pauses
- It only takes over while at least two of its streams are running. A stream that drops leaves the picture after 3 seconds and is back in it once it reconnects. The others don't freeze with it, even when it's the one on Telescope
- The picture runs at the frame rate of the stream going to Telescope. Its size is the canvas size under Video in Settings, or on Auto the size the Telescope camera opened at

## 3D from two phones

An optional early version that's still in Lab (switch it on under **Streaming and devices** in the Plugins tab of Settings). It turns two phones streaming at the same time into one 3D picture on the **Telescope** camera. Both phones need the newer phone app for the timing to match. Two phones of the same model work best, since different models differ in field of view, colour and frame rate.

- The switch on the **3D** card turns it on. It only takes over the Telescope camera while two streams run. The first stream you started is the left eye and **Swap eyes** exchanges them when the picture looks inside out
- **Look** is **Side by side** (for a headset or a 3D screen) or **Red/cyan** (for glasses, mixed with the Dubois matrices to get colours closer to the original than with a plain red and cyan split)
- Side by side gives each eye half of the picture's width. On a 16:9 canvas that squeezes each eye sideways, which is how half side by side works. Set a 32:9 canvas (3840x1080 for example) under Video in Settings for full side by side with nothing squeezed
- **Fine tune** opens the sliders that line the two pictures up by hand. **Depth** slides the two pictures against each other (up to 15 % of the width) to change how far forward the 3D reaches. **Height** lifts or lowers the right eye (up to 15 %) for phones that don't sit level. **Size** (80 to 125 %) zooms in on the phone that sees the wider view so both pictures show the same thing. Over 100 % it zooms the right eye and under 100 % the left one. Neither eye is ever shrunk into black borders. **Left tilt** and **Right tilt** each roll one eye (up to 5 degrees). Depth, Height and the tilts zoom both eyes a little, by the same amount to avoid any black edge or corner. **Timing** moves the right eye up to 200 ms against the left one, for a phone whose picture runs late. A positive value pairs each left picture with a later one from the right phone and a negative value with an earlier one. Without capture times (see below) the left picture is delayed for a positive value and the right one for a negative value instead. Double-click a slider to reset it
- **Size is worked out from the lenses** when both phones tell Telescope which lens they use. The first time a pair of phones runs with Size at 100 %, it is set from the ratio of the two lenses' fields of view (a 26 mm lens on the left against a 24 mm one on the right gives about 108 %, which zooms the right eye). **Swap eyes** turns Size round (108 % becomes 92 %) to give each phone the zoom it had. Swapping back gives the exact size you started from. It is left alone when the lenses are too far apart for Size to fix, like a wide lens against a telephoto. Use the same zoom on both phones (the phones' own zoom isn't counted)
- **Match** evens out two different phones (two of the same model need little of it). It puts both phones on manual exposure with the same shutter, the left phone's if it is manual already and otherwise 1/60 s (limited to what the frame rate allows). Then it nudges the right phone's ISO over a few seconds until the middle of both pictures is within about 3 % as bright. A left phone that was on automatic is brought back to the brightness its own picture had before Match to stop the picture jumping. Last it fits a colour curve for each channel from the middle of the two pictures and applies it to the right eye while the picture is packed, which costs next to nothing. Phones that can't take a manual exposure only get the colour curve
- **Brightness** (under Match, in stops, up to 2 either way) moves both phones together once a Match with manual exposure is done. It scales both ISOs by the same factor and stops where either lens runs out of ISO range. It is off until then. The card's reset button sets it to 0 and the phones to the ISO Match gave them.
- The badge in the card's header (it shows one at a time, whatever needs a look first) reads **Matched**, **Partly matched** (an ISO ran out of range or the two phones have no shutter speed in common) or **Match failed** (a phone didn't answer). **Reset match** drops the colour curve. The exposure Match gave the phones is left as it is until you change it on the Camera card. The curve also goes when the eyes change (Swap eyes, or a stream stopping). Match needs the same light on both phones and something in the middle of both pictures to read
- The reset button in the card's header resets the look, the eye order and the Fine tune sliders to their defaults and drops the match. The switch and the phones' exposure are left as they are
- When a stream stalls for a few seconds the picture goes on from the other phone alone, shown flat. The badge reads **Waiting** until both are sending again. Pictures saved from before a Blur or any other effect changed are never used, to stop anything clear slipping into the picture
- Each eye can be refined with that phone's own **Transforms** and **Colour grading** cards. Click its tile and adjust. This only adds delay, set by the slower eye, since every stream is processed before 3D sees it and pairing goes by capture time. Effects that act separately on each stream (Auto framing, Background, auto colour grading and Fun pack looks) make the two eyes disagree. Leave them off or set them the same on both
- Pictures are paired by when the cameras took them and not by when they arrived. The badge shows **In sync** when the two are within half a frame of each other (counting how well the phone and computer clocks agree), **Rough sync** when they're a bit further apart and **Not synced** when it can't tell, like with an older phone app, a webcam or the Browser camera. Then the newest picture of each goes together
- The picture is made at the frame rate of the stream going to Telescope and at the canvas size under Video in Settings (on Auto, the size the Telescope camera opened at). Two pictures get scaled and packed for every frame, which costs some processor time
- Leave **One picture** off while 3D is on. 3D runs first and One picture then draws its layout from what 3D made. The first stream's tile in it is the finished 3D picture next to the others, instead of 3D alone

### In a headset

The **Headset** switch on the 3D card (off by default) shows the 3D picture in a headset's browser, with each eye getting its own half. It needs 3D on and two streams running.

1. Switch on **Headset**. The card shows a code, the link and its address
2. On the headset, on the same Wi-Fi as this computer, open the link in the browser (type it in, or send it to the headset from a phone). The browser warns that the connection isn't private because the certificate is this computer's own. Accept it once
3. Press **Enter VR**

The picture hangs about 2 metres in front of you, at 16:9 and about 2.5 metres wide. Before you press Enter VR the page shows the same picture flat, with a line saying whether it is connected.

To leave VR, press B or Y on a controller or squeeze a grip. The trigger alone does nothing. A stray press can't throw you out. Press A or X to place the picture in front of you again.

- Both eyes go into one JPEG up to 1920 x 1080 at up to 30 frames a second. Each eye is squeezed to half the width, as the camera's side by side does. The page stretches it back. That is roughly 30 to 60 Mbit/s depending on the picture, which needs Wi-Fi 5 or newer. Nothing is made while no headset is connected
- The link is the same across restarts until you press **New link**. It lives in its own file next to your settings, away from presets and anything you share
- It is the same picture the Telescope camera gets, with Fine tune and Match. Privacy blur and the Wait screen apply to it too. A headset that falls behind skips to the newest picture. Telescope sends the next one once the headset has shown the last. The headset goes black after 3 seconds without a new one. Two headsets can watch at once
- The link has a secret in it that others on the network don't have. **New link** makes another and disconnects the old one. After Telescope restarts the link is the same but the switch is off
- It listens on port 7484 by default (the link shows another free port if that one is taken) while the switch is on, 3D is on and the plugin is running. A firewall has to let through the port shown in the link
